# How a jet engine works - one narration line per line
How does a jet engine push a plane across an ocean? It is mostly one very clever tube.
First, a giant fan sucks in air. Most of it simply flows around the outside.
The rest dives into the compressor, where rows of blades squeeze it to a fraction of its size.
Squeezed air is hot and dense, which is exactly what fuel wants.
In the combustor, fuel sprays in and burns, and the gas blasts toward the back.
That blast spins the turbine, blades on the same shaft as the fan.
So the engine feeds itself. The exhaust that pushes you forward also powers the intake.
Out the back it roars, and Newton does the rest. Push gas back, and the plane goes forward.
Fan, squeeze, burn, blast. That is every jet engine, in four words.
